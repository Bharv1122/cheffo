# Galvan's Eatery & Beer Garden — Proposal Structure

Working notes for a website proposal to Galvan's Eatery & Beer Garden, 500 E Cotati Ave, Cotati CA.
Family-run (brothers Ivan and Omar Galvan), three food trucks plus a new brick-and-mortar beer garden.

## What we know going in

- Three red food trucks since 2020, popping up at breweries (Shady Oak, Old Caz, Cooperage, HenHouse).
- First permanent location in the former Jaded Toad, in the Oliver's center in downtown Cotati.
- Positioned as a game-day hangout: communal picnic tables, big-screen TVs, food built for lingering.
- Known for quesabirria, carne asada fries, street tacos, burritos; kid-friendly items on the menu.
- Active on Instagram (`@galvanseatery`, profile name "The Trailer - 1,2&3 & Beer Garden") and Facebook.
- **Their listings have not caught up with the move.** The Facebook page is still labeled Rohnert Park,
  and menu aggregators still file them under Rohnert Park 94928, while the beer garden is Cotati 94931.

Lead with that last point. The pitch is not "you need a website" — it is "your brick-and-mortar is new
and half the internet still points at the old place." That is a problem an owner can feel.

## Working assumptions (unconfirmed — the check-up settles them)

- **They're on Square.** A guess, but a good one: they came out of food trucks, where Square is close
  to universal. If it holds, the ordering stage is configuration rather than construction. If they're
  on Toast or Clover the shape changes slightly; if there is genuinely nothing, the price roughly
  triples. Do not quote the ordering stage before confirming this.
- Some DoorDash/Uber Eats presence at standard 25–30% commission.
- Google Business Profile unclaimed or unmanaged.

Square's own plan costs are separate and go to Square. Don't quote them from memory in anything
client-facing — confirm the current rates with the owners during the check-up.

## Structure to pitch

### Step 0 — Digital Check-Up — no charge

60–90 minutes on site. Establish: who owns the domain (if any), whether the Google Business Profile is
claimed, what POS they run, whether online ordering already exists, who runs Instagram, and whether a
half-built Square or Wix site is sitting somewhere. Output is a one-page findings sheet.

This de-risks scope, and it usually sells the job on its own.

### Tier 1 — Get Found & Get Visited — $2,800–$4,000

Mobile-first site: menu, hours, location, photos, large-party and catering inquiry, links to social,
maps, and review profiles. Plus, and this is the part that carries the tier:

- Google Business Profile claimed, corrected, and optimized (hours, category, photos, menu link).
- Name/address/phone consistency across Google, Apple Maps, Yelp, Facebook, Bing.
- A truck schedule section — they run three trucks *and* the beer garden. "Where's the truck this
  weekend?" is one of their most common questions; a site that can't answer it fails.
- The questions people actually ask: game days and TVs, dog-friendly, kid-friendly, parking, whether
  the beer garden is ever 21+, large-group policy.

Route the inquiry form to **text**, not email. Family restaurants do not check inboxes, and a catering
lead that dies in a Gmail folder is worse than no form at all.

### Add-on — Online Ordering — $450–$900 on Square, $1,200–$2,400 from scratch

Split into two honest paths:

- **Connect existing.** They're on Toast/Square and ordering is already in the box. A few hours of
  configuration plus routing traffic to it. Priced small.
- **Set up new.** No ordering today. Real project: menu build, modifiers, taxes, printer routing, staff
  training. This is an operations project that touches their kitchen, not a web project. Priced real.

The value story that lands is commission. If they're doing meaningful DoorDash/Uber volume at 25–30%,
moving even a third of it to a first-party page is real monthly money. Put their actual numbers in.

Do not oversell it. A beer garden's money is on-premise; ordering is convenience for the pickup crowd.

### Tier 2 — Keep Them Coming Back — +$1,600–$2,800

- Owner-editable menu, hours, and events. Taps and specials change constantly; if they can't update it
  themselves the site is stale in 90 days and we own the blame.
- Events calendar — game days, live music, specials. This is the beer garden's actual traffic driver.
- Email/SMS capture plus one send a month. Owned audience instead of rented reach; highest-ROI growth
  lever available to an independent restaurant.
- Review loop: QR at the table pointing to Google, plus a response cadence for the owner.
- One-page monthly numbers: GBP calls, direction requests, website taps, first-party order count and
  average ticket, list size — with one sentence on what to change next month.
- Static FAQ page. No chatbot.

### Care Plan — $85–$175/month, offered with every tier

Hosting, domain renewal, updates, holiday hours, listing monitoring, the monthly one-pager. Small money,
but it is what keeps the site true and keeps the relationship alive.

### Add-ons

- Food photography, half-day — **$450–$850**. Highest-impact money on the whole list.
- Spanish menu, or fully bilingual site — **$350–$800**.
- Table cards and QR codes — **$150–$300**, printing at cost.

Keep these off the main number so the core decision stays a simple yes or no.

## On the pricing

Ranges, not fixed numbers, because scope genuinely isn't settled until the check-up. Say that out
loud — it converts a vague range into an honest one: *"the range becomes a number at the check-up,
and then it doesn't move."*

The bands assume a solo operator in Sonoma County pitching a business that is paying commercial rent
for the first time. If they flinch, phase the work rather than discounting it — a discount teaches
them the first number was fake.

## Deliberately excluded

- **AI phone answering.** Revisit only after we understand call volume and who answers today. An accurate
  GBP and a good FAQ page kill most "are you open / do you have space" calls with no AI at all.
- **Reservations / OpenTable.** Walk-in beer garden. Large-party inquiry only.
- **Loyalty app, blog, SEO content program.** No one at a taqueria is writing blog posts.
- **Custom-built ordering or anything touching payments.** Use an established platform.
- **Anything requiring weekly owner effort.** It will not happen, and the site will rot.

## Commercial terms to state plainly

- They own the domain, the Google Business Profile, the ordering account, and the hosting. Say it in
  writing. Every restaurant owner has heard the story about the web guy who vanished with the domain.
- No 12-month lock-in. No percentage-of-sales pricing. 50/50 deposit and delivery.
- Photography and Spanish translation quoted separately so the base number stays easy to say yes to.

## How to present it

Do not send a text proposal. Mock their homepage on a phone using their real Instagram photos, real menu,
and real hours, and show it to them at a picnic table. For a restaurant owner, seeing it beats reading
about it — and it lets the listings problem land in the same breath.
