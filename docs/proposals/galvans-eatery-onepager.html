Where things stand today

The beer garden is open and it's already becoming a game-day spot in town. The internet hasn't quite caught up with you. Between three trucks and a permanent address, there is more than one Galvan's floating around out there — your Facebook page still carries the Rohnert Park label from the truck days, and menu sites have you filed under an address you don't serve from.

Nobody's getting badly lost over that; Cotati and Rohnert Park are the same few square miles. The cost is quieter. Google ranks local restaurants partly on how consistently the same name, address, and phone turn up across the web — when yours disagree, you rank lower than you've earned on the search that matters most, which is somebody two blocks away typing "tacos near me." Reviews can end up split across two records instead of stacking up on one.

Underneath all of it is the question you get constantly and can't currently answer in one place: which Galvan's is which, and where are the trucks this weekend.

Every bit of this is fixable in weeks, not months. None of it requires you to become a computer person.

Online Ordering

If you're on Square, you are almost certainly already paying for ordering tools you haven't switched on. That's a configuration job, and I'm not going to price it like a construction project.

  • Orders routed so they land in the kitchen the way your line actually works

If you're not on Square, or there's genuinely nothing in place, this becomes a real build — roughly $1,200–$2,400. The Check-Up tells us which of the two we're looking at before either of us commits to a number.

Whatever you pay Square is separate, and it goes to Square, not to me. We'll confirm your exact plan and rates together during the Check-Up. The point of this stage is simple: every order that comes through your own page instead of a delivery app keeps roughly a quarter to a third more of the ticket in your pocket.

What I'm not selling you

Things that get pitched to restaurants constantly and would be wrong for yours right now.

An AI phone answerer
Maybe someday. First we'd need to know how many calls you're actually missing, and a correct Google listing kills most of them for free.
A reservation system
You're a walk-in beer garden with picnic tables. A big-party inquiry form covers what you need.
A loyalty app
Nobody downloads a taqueria app. An email and text list does the same job for a fraction of the cost.
A blog or SEO package
You're running three trucks and a kitchen. You are never going to write blog posts, and you shouldn't have to.
